How TFT Esports Works: From Ranked to Worlds

Every TFT set ends with a World Championship. Here is the road there – explained with Set 18 (Enchanted Wilds) in EMEA – and what you need to compete yourself.

TFT E-Sports editors 10 Nov 2026 6 min read

Competitive Teamfight Tactics runs in cycles: every set gets its own season of official tournaments, and it ends with the Tactician's Crown, the World Championship. Riot organizes the official events by region – Americas, EMEA and APAC – while China qualifies through its own local competition. Next to that there are many smaller, unofficial cups that are organized for one country or language.

The road to Worlds has five steps. We explain them with the Set 18 season in EMEA; the structure is very similar in the other regions.

TFT Compete Card: how ranked leads to tournaments

Everything starts on the ranked ladder. To play official tournaments you need a Compete Card on competetft.com, and you have to sign up for each qualifier before the deadline. For the first Tactician's Trials of Set 18 the card had to be set up by 5 September.

There is no fixed minimum rank. Instead, Riot takes a ladder snapshot and gives the slots to the highest-placed players who signed up. For Trials 1 in Set 18 that meant 248 slots on EUW, 51 on EUNE and 21 on TR.

What are the TFT Tactician's Trials?

The Tactician's Trials are the official tournaments that ranked players can actually reach. In Set 18 EMEA had two of them, in September and October, each played over two weekends:

  • 256 players start, with 4 games per day and reseeding between days.
  • The field gets smaller every day until a final lobby of 8 players.
  • The top 8 qualify for the Regional Finals; in Trials 2 the top 4 by Qualifier Points from the last two days also reach the Play-Ins.
  • Each Trials event had a prize pool of $12,500.

Depending on your snapshot place you start on Day 1 or Day 3. Age and residency rules are in the official rulebook on competetft.com.

What is the TFT Pro Circuit?

The TFT Pro Circuit is a series of invite-only cups for a region's best players, played in parallel to the Trials. In Set 18 that was three cups – Riftbeast Cup, Elderwood Cup and Blossom Cup, $30,200 each. Only the region's top 32 players from the previous set are invited.

  • Day 1: 32 players, 6 games.
  • Day 2: three stages.
  • Day 3: a checkmate final with 8 players – the first player who reaches 20 points and then wins a game takes the title.

The winner of each cup gets a direct slot at the Tactician's Crown. All results also award Pro Points, and the region's top Pro Points earner qualifies directly as well. The Elderwood Cup was a cross-region cup in which EMEA played against AMER.

How do the TFT Regional Finals work?

The Regional Finals bring the qualified players together: 64 players per region from the Tactician's Trials, the Pro Circuit and Ladder Points, played over two weekends. According to topmeta.gg, the Set 18 Regional Finals of AMER, EMEA and APAC (192 players in total) decided 17 Tactician's Crown slots: 7 for APAC, 5 for AMER and 5 for EMEA. China sends 11 players, qualified through its own events and the Chinese Pro Circuit, according to Riot's primer.

The EMEA Grand Finals were won by M8 L3S Coco with 34 points, just ahead of Yakumo (33) and MIH bensac (32). See the full standings.

Tactician's Crown: how the TFT World Championship works

At the end of the set, 40 players from all regions meet at the Tactician's Crown. In Set 18 it runs from 13 to 15 November 2026 with a prize pool of $470,000. Day 1 cuts the field from 40 to 32, Day 2 plays down to the final eight, and the Grand Final uses the checkmate format again. Read our Tactician's Crown guide for the schedule, the qualified players and the stream.

Unofficial TFT cups: where most players start

Besides Riot's circuit there are official side events such as the Tactician's Superbrawl, a 4v4 tournament, and a lot of community tournaments: national championships, streamer cups and league formats – for example the TFT DACH Championship for Germany, Austria and Switzerland. They are the easiest way to get tournament experience.

In our tournament calendar you can pick your country under “Where do you play?”. The calendar then only shows what you can enter: the global events, the official events of your region and the cups for your country.

How to get started in competitive TFT

Start with your Compete Card, keep an eye on the snapshot dates and gain experience in community cups:

  • Set up your Compete Card early – the deadline is before the first Trials of a set.
  • Watch the snapshot dates. Your ladder place on that day decides whether you get a slot.
  • Play community cups to get used to tournament lobbies and point systems.
  • Climb with a plan. Current comps, tier lists and coaching are on TFTClimb.
